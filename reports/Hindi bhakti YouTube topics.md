# Aarti Aangan should chant, explain, and time festivals

**Read this first: the research tools were blocked from youtube.com, Wikipedia, support.google.com and several calendar sites, so every view count below is an approximate figure from a search-result snippet, not a verified number.** With that caveat, the evidence points one way. Millions of views on Hindi bhakti YouTube come from a few evergreen, text-based devotional staples (Hanuman Chalisa, aartis, Shiva and Krishna mantras and bhajans) that legacy labels and famous singers have owned for a decade or more. The one clearly sourced mega-hit is T-Series' Hanuman Chalisa (Hariharan), reported at **4.6B to 5B+ views**. A new channel cannot beat those uploads on head terms. Aarti Aangan's better route is to start with the 108-times jaap format it already plans, differentiate it (a real voice, meaning on screen, a specific weekday or festival, a niche sound design), and publish ahead of the Sharad Navratri to Diwali peak that starts in about ten days. The riskiest move is the most obvious one: a still deity image with a looped chant, which matches YouTube's "inauthentic content" pattern and may block monetization. The sections below rank topics, give a Sep 2026 to early 2027 calendar, and list copyright and monetization cautions. Section 4 lists what to verify by hand.

## What the evidence shows about millions-of-views bhakti content

The single verified-by-snippet outlier is Hanuman Chalisa (Gulshan Kumar, Hariharan) on T-Series Bhakti Sagar. It is described as the first devotional video past 1B views and the most-viewed Indian video on YouTube. Reported counts differ by source and date: 3B in an older Swarajya headline ([Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/culture/three-billion-views-for-hanuman-chalisa-on-t-series-youtube-channel-how-hindu-devotional-songs-conquered-the-internet)), 4.6B with 20M likes ([India.com](https://www.india.com/entertainment/the-most-viewed-devotional-song-on-youtube-has-over-4-6-billion-views-has-20-million-likes-song-is-hanuman-chalisa-singer-is-hariharan-7885900/)), and "5 Billion+" in the video's own title ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EAcPNbtV7XA)). One search summary quoted 5.75B, which conflicts with the others and is unconfirmed. I found no reliable counts for Shiv Tandav, Mahamrityunjay, Gayatri, Ganesh Aarti, Durga Chalisa or Krishna bhajans. The only other figure was about 4.8M views for one upload of Anuradha Paudwal's "Om Jai Jagdish Hare", which is low confidence because other uploads are likely far larger ([kworb](https://kworb.net/youtube/video/3ucCEjXS9n8.html)). Which topics sit in the 100M to 1B tier is therefore an inference, not a finding.

Channel-level data is more useful. T-Series Bhakti Sagar has roughly **79 to 80M subscribers** and close to 28B views ([Essential Gyaan](https://essentialgyaan.com/top-devotional-bhakti-youtube-channels-in-india/)). A second tier holds 8 to 17M: Bhajan Marg at about 17M, Bhajan India and Spiritual Mantra at 10M+, and Saregama Bhakti at about 8M ([HypeAuditor](https://hypeauditor.com/youtube/UCEk1jBxAl6fe-_G37G7huQA/), [ThoughtLeaders](https://app.thoughtleaders.io/youtube/saregama-bhakti)). The most instructive case for a small channel is Bhajan Marg. It grows through daily long-form satsang and Q&A, not music, adding about **200K subscribers and 156M views in 30 days** at roughly 24 videos a month ([HypeAuditor](https://hypeauditor.com/youtube/UCEk1jBxAl6fe-_G37G7huQA/)). Explanation and spoken guidance are a growth engine, and they also sidestep music-licensing problems. The notes found no reliable data on small, fast-growing channels, the share of 1-hour loops, or Shorts performance for mantras, so treat claims about those formats as hypotheses.

## Mantra jaap search results are crowded by old uploads, so niche packaging is the opening

For "108 times" searches, results are dominated by established names. Om Namah Shivay 108 shows Hariharan, Anuradha Paudwal (2013/2014 uploads) and Kripa Record alongside meditation channels with 2016, 2025 and 2026 uploads ([Hariharan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WiVJLFRbFSw), [Paudwal](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c2UAWr5HuPU)). Mahamrityunjay 108 shows Shankar Sahney, Suresh Wadkar, Paudwal and a "21 Brahmins" version ([Sahney](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BMDLRW1iDpI), [Wadkar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zTY13rtKMAs), [21 Brahmins](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZDuhp5o8Ybw)). No view counts were readable, but uploads from 2012 to 2017 have had a decade to accumulate views, so I infer they hold the top positions.

The gaps in the results are the opportunity. Ram naam 108 already has packaging variants: "108 times in 3 minutes super fast", "only vocals and ocean waves" and a Ram Navami special ([3-minute](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xK2ekMrV3Dg), [ocean waves](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PVnitAxrY8A), [Ram Navami](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kScxvPdCT4w)). For **Hare Krishna Mahamantra**, the results showed a 25-minute "[2026]" upload, a 30-minute version, a 432Hz version, a choir version and a live japa stream. **No 1-hour, 3-hour or sleep version appeared** ([25 min](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mpkpI_eUNvg), [30 min](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1bySQZdQmmk), [432Hz](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-aEgcRZLfpI), [live](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zJ-zrcugMzc)). That absence may mean unmet demand or may mean the format performs poorly; the notes cannot tell which. Test it with one video.

Observed title patterns are consistent. The mantra and count come first ("108 times / 108 बार"), with Hindi and English mixed, then a benefit or occasion tag such as "Monday Special", "Ram Navami Special", "for Peace Positive Energy & Meditation", a duration, a year, or "with meaning/subtitles" ([example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4egcz2ch52I), [Monday Special](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YsrkW_p3eF0), [with meaning](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L-y1sr1qUlE)). Other formats seen in titles are lyrical videos with Hindi and English lyrics, 8D audio, live-stream aarti and Shorts ([lyrical](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NE3SWh9_vR4), [8D](https://m.youtube.com/watch?v=BP0VyfkDDEI), [live](https://www.youtube.com/live/Q-CvXI3YbtE)).

## Copyright and monetization: the recording, the picture, and the loop are the risks

This section is not legal advice, and none of the official YouTube or statute pages could be opened.

**Text versus recording.** Indian works last the author's life plus 60 years ([lawgist s.22](https://lawgist.in/copyright-act/22)), so Tulsidas, Kabir, Mirabai, Vedic mantras and Phillauri's "Om Jai Jagdish Hare" text (Phillauri died in 1881) are free to use ([Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q15669279)). Sound recordings are separately protected for 60 years from the following year ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/copyright/1189422/playing-sound-recordings-in-marriages-social-festivities-whether-copyright-infringement-or-not)), so every label or famous-singer version is off limits without a licence. The melody is a separate musical work too, so sing a traditional or original tune rather than copying a well-known recorded one. Content ID claims both recordings and compositions, and a claim on a cover usually leaves the video up but sends the ad revenue to the rights holder ([StemSplit](https://stemsplit.io/blog/cover-songs-youtube-permission)). A 2019 claim against a reggae Hanuman Chalisa track shows that even public-domain text can be claimed through arrangement ([summary](https://hanumanchalisaa.com/disclaimer/)). The religious-ceremony exception in Section 52(1)(za) covers ceremonies, not YouTube uploads. Modern translations, commentary and later-added aarti verses are also protected, so check the exact text used.

**Images and audio.** Pictures of deities from Google Images are usually copyrighted and risk a takedown, not just a claim. Raja Ravi Varma paintings (he died in 1906) are public domain and are on Wikimedia Commons ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Raja_Ravi_Varma_-_Dattatreya.jpg)); check each file page. Pexels is free for commercial use without attribution ([Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/license/)), and Freesound needs CC0 or CC BY, never NC, for a monetized channel ([Freesound FAQ](https://freesound.org/help/faq/)). Pixabay tracks can still trigger Content ID claims, which the Pixabay licence certificate can dispute ([Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/blog/posts/how-to-clear-a-youtube-content-id-claim-with-a-pix-190/)). Do not rely on "fair use" disclaimers; that is my general knowledge, unsourced. Keep raw recordings and licence receipts as dispute evidence.

**Partner Program thresholds.** Secondary guides describe two tiers: an entry tier at 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads and 3,000 watch hours or 3M Shorts views, giving fan funding only, and a full ad tier at **1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours or 10M Shorts views** ([AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-partner-program-requirements-2026-the-complete-guide)). One secondary source reports that new applicants will need 8,000 watch hours or 20M Shorts views from 1 Feb 2027; this is unverified and matters if Aarti Aangan expects to qualify near that date.

**The loop problem.** Since 15 July 2025 YouTube's "inauthentic content" wording targets mass-produced, templated videos, including looping visuals or static images that are nearly identical across videos ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/), [SubSub](https://www.subsub.io/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-2025)). Ambient and meditation channels are reported to have some of the highest rejection rates, though that statistic comes from a commercial blog ([Dynamoi](https://dynamoi.com/learn/youtube-music-promotion/ambient-music-channel-monetization-rules)). A 108x chant on one still image is exactly that pattern. Reduce the risk with your own voice, unique visuals for each video, on-screen meaning or translation, a spoken intro, and varied lengths and structures. Include explanatory videos (rank 6). On AI, stylized artwork of a deity is probably exempt from the disclosure toggle, while photorealistic AI scenes or a cloned voice of a known singer need disclosure or should be avoided ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content/)); the exemption details come from conflicting secondary sources.

## What to check yourself before committing

Because none of the view figures were verified, treat the ranking as a hypothesis and test it. First, on YouTube search, type each mantra ("Hare Krishna Mahamantra 108", "Mahamrityunjay 108", "Hanuman Chalisa meaning") and filter by view count; note upload year, length and whether top results have a meaning or subtitle layer. Second, use vidIQ (or Social Blade) to see search volume, competition and top-video stats for these keywords and for Bhajan Marg, Saregama Bhakti and any small competitor channels. Third, confirm every date on Drik Panchang, especially Navratri, Dev Deepawali, Gita Jayanti and the January to March 2027 Ekadashis. Fourth, read YouTube's own pages in YouTube Studio and Help: the Partner Program thresholds (particularly the reported February 2027 change), the inauthentic and reused content policy, and the altered or synthetic content disclosure. Fifth, check the Copyright Act text on s.38 and s.27 terms if you plan anything near old recordings.

## Conclusion

The notes show that the popular question, "which topic gets millions of views", has a lopsided answer: a handful of legacy uploads earn billions, and a small channel gets nothing from imitating them. What a new channel can own is packaging and trust, meaning a specific festival or weekday, a length the incumbents skipped, and a spoken explanation that shows a person made it. That same packaging is what YouTube's monetization reviewers reward, so growth strategy and policy strategy point the same way.

Aarti Aangan's most valuable first experiment is small: publish the Hare Krishna Mahamantra in a 108-count cut and a 1-hour cut with meaning on screen, then compare each video's watch time. If long versions hold viewers, add the Navratri and Diwali mantras immediately; if they do not, shift toward explained and shorter formats, where Bhajan Marg's growth shows the demand is.
