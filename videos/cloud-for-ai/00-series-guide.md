# Cloud for AI Engineers: video series guide

Goal: a total beginner understands every video. No prior cloud or AI knowledge needed.

## The 5 rules for every video
1. **One video, one big idea.** Everything else is a supporting detail.
2. **Analogy first, jargon second.** Say the everyday version, then give the real term once and never rush past it.
3. **Every term is explained the first time it appears.** If you can't explain it in one sentence, cut it.
4. **Numbers only when they make a point.** Pick 2 or 3 per video. Round them ("about 10 times slower").
5. **End with a 3-line recap and one question for the viewer to answer.**

## Standard video shape (6 to 8 minutes)
| Part | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0:00-0:20 | A real problem the viewer has ("Why did my GPU bill hit $5,000?") |
| The picture | 0:20-1:30 | One analogy that frames the whole topic |
| Idea 1, 2, 3 | 1:30-6:00 | Each idea: analogy, real term, tiny example, on-screen diagram |
| Recap | 6:00-6:45 | Three sentences, same words as the analogy |
| Check yourself | 6:45-7:15 | One question; answer in the pinned comment |
| Next video | 7:15-7:30 | One-line teaser linking to the next topic |

## Order to publish (each video uses the one before it)
0. **Intro / Big Picture** (what "cloud for AI" means: the whole series as one story)
1. Compute (the chips you rent)
2. Containers (packing your app in a box)
3. Data and storage (where information lives)
4. Training (teaching the model)
5. Inference (using the model, fast)
6. MLOps (shipping it safely, again and again)
7. GenAI apps (RAG and agents)
8. Security (keeping it safe)
9. Cost (not going broke)
10. Learning path (what to do next)

Rule of thumb for the series: videos 1-3 are the **kitchen** (equipment and ingredients), 4-5 are **cooking and serving**, 6-8 are **running the restaurant**, 9 is the **bill**, 10 is **your career**.

## The one analogy that runs through the whole series: a restaurant
| Restaurant | Cloud for AI |
|---|---|
| Kitchen equipment (ovens) | GPUs and other chips |
| Meal-kit box with everything inside | Container |
| Head chef assigning cooks | Kubernetes (orchestrator) |
| Pantry and fridge | Storage and databases |
| Training a new chef | Training the model |
| Serving a customer | Inference |
| Standard recipes + quality checks | MLOps |
| Menu with fresh specials from a cookbook | RAG |
| Locks, staff badges, health rules | Security |
| Food cost and wasted ingredients | Cost / FinOps |

Use it lightly. If it gets forced, drop it for that video.

## Visuals checklist
- Animated diagram for every "how it moves" idea (data, requests, GPUs talking).
- Big text for the one key number or term on screen while you say it.
- Never show a full spec table. Show one row, highlight one cell.
- Reuse the notebook look (ruled paper, highlighter) as your brand; screenshots of the notes work well as B-roll.

## Notes accuracy
The notebook is dated 14 Oct 2026. Product names, prices and chip availability change fast. Say "as of October 2026" on screen whenever you show a price, a chip name or a rule date, and re-check before recording.
