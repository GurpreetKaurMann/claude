# Copyright, Licensing and Monetization for a New Hindi Bhakti YouTube Channel (India, as of Sept 2026)

NOT LEGAL ADVICE. Research notes for a report writer. Important limitation: support.google.com (YouTube Help), blog.youtube, Wikipedia and several other pages were blocked by the fetch proxy during research, so YouTube policy facts below come from web-search result summaries and secondary creator-education sites (search summaries link the official Help pages, but I could not open them). Verify all YPP numbers and policy wording on the official pages in YouTube Studio before acting.

## 1. What is public domain in India vs protected

### Takeaway
Old texts (Tulsidas, Kabir, Mirabai, Vedic mantras, Phillauri's aarti) are out of copyright because Indian literary/musical works last author's life + 60 years. But every recording (label or singer), every modern tune, arrangement, translation and newly written bhajan has its own copyright, so "public-domain lyrics" never make a T-Series/Saregama audio free to reuse.

### Cited Findings
- Literary, dramatic, musical or artistic works published in the author's lifetime are protected until 60 years from the beginning of the calendar year after the author's death (Copyright Act 1957, s.22) — [lawgist s.22](https://lawgist.in/copyright-act/22)
- Sound recordings are protected for 60 years from the start of the calendar year following publication (s.27) — [Mondaq/search summary of s.27](https://www.mondaq.com/india/copyright/1189422/playing-sound-recordings-in-marriages-social-festivities-whether-copyright-infringement-or-not); statute overview at [Copyright Office handbook](https://copyright.gov.in/documents/handbook.html)
- Performer's right lasts 50 years from the start of the calendar year after the performance (s.38), per the search summary — [Kamlatech s.38](https://ip.kamlatech.in/The%20Copyright%20Act%201957/section_38). (Note: the Act was amended in 2012; the 50-year figure is what the source shows, verify current text.)
- Hanuman Chalisa text by Tulsidas is described as public domain, but individual arrangements/recordings can be and are claimed (example: a 2019 claim against a reggae Hanuman Chalisa track) — [Hanuman Chalisa disclaimer/summary results](https://hanumanchalisaa.com/disclaimer/), [Wikipedia: Hanuman Chalisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanuman_Chalisa) (search summary only; not opened)
- "Om Jai Jagdish Hare" was written by Shardha Ram Phillauri c. 1870; he died 24 June 1881, so the text is far outside the 60-year term. Wikidata lists it as public domain (in the US) — [Wikidata Q15669279](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q15669279), [Wikipedia: Shardha Ram Phillauri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shardha_Ram_Phillauri). Caution: many popular recorded versions (Anuradha Paudwal, Lata etc.) are protected recordings.
- Ancient/public-domain bhajans cannot be copyrighted unless there is significant original arrangement or interpretation; a derivative work needs substantial transformation bearing the author's personality — [BusinessMitra on bhajan/aarti copyright](https://businessmitra.com/blogs/copyright-registration-of-bhajan-and-arti), [Wikipedia: Derivative work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivative_work) (secondary sources)
- Tulsidas (d. 1623), Kabir, Mirabai (16th c.) are historic authors; Mirabai's compositions are still sung as Krishna bhajans — [Wikipedia: Mirabai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirabai). Raja Ravi Varma (d. 1906) paintings of deities are public domain and hosted on Commons — [Commons: Kali by Raja Ravi Varma](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kali_by_Raja_Ravi_Varma.jpg)
- Section 52(1)(za): performance of a work or communication of a sound recording in a bona fide religious ceremony is not infringement; Copyright Office 2019 notice reaffirmed this for religious ceremonies/weddings. This concerns live/ceremonial use, NOT uploading to YouTube — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/copyright/1189422/playing-sound-recordings-in-marriages-social-festivities-whether-copyright-infringement-or-not), [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/does-playing-music-in-marriages-and-religious-ceremonies-amount-to-copyright-infringement/)
- T-Series mostly claims albums it bought or produced, not the underlying ancient texts — [Quora thread (low-quality source)](https://www.quora.com/Why-does-T-Series-claim-copyright-for-old-Hindu-devotional-songs-and-mantras)

### Inferences
- Likely safe to base content on (text): Hanuman Chalisa, Ramcharitmanas verses, Kabir dohas, Mirabai padas, Vedic mantras (Gayatri, Mahamrityunjaya, etc.), Phillauri's Om Jai Jagdish Hare original text. Sung and recorded by the creator (own voice/instruments), the new recording is the creator's own.
- Likely protected/avoid without licence: film songs and film bhajans, label albums, modern composed bhajans (even to traditional deity themes), popular singers' recordings, well-known tunes composed by living/recent composers (tune is a separate musical work), modern translations/commentaries (e.g., a published Hindi/English translation), and later-added verses/aarti stanzas by modern authors. Many "traditional" aartis circulate in versions with modern added lines; check the exact text used.
- Ambiguity: the Tulsidas text is safe, but the melody a creator sings may be a label's composed tune; sing a traditional/own tune, not a copy of a famous recorded one.
- The age of a recording matters: a 60-year term for recordings means only pre-c.1965 (publication) recordings could now be out of term, and this is hard to verify; treat all label recordings as protected.

### Gaps
- Could not open primary statute text or case law; no authoritative source on whether a specific modern edition (Gita Press text) of Hanuman Chalisa is claimed. Author-death dates for lesser-known aarti writers not researched. Exact current s.38 term not verified against the statute.

## 2. YouTube Content ID, covers, others' audio, Google images, stock music

### Takeaway
Content ID matches both the sound recording and the underlying composition. Your own fresh recording of public-domain text should be clean, but a modern melody, someone's karaoke/track, or stray label audio triggers claims that divert ad revenue (or block/strike). Claims are not strikes, but repeated misuse and false disputes risk channel problems.

### Cited Findings
- Content ID scans uploads against a database of rights-holder reference files and places a claim on a match — [Uppbeat: Content ID explained](https://uppbeat.io/blog/youtube/youtube-content-id)
- Sound recording claims arise from using another's recording (karaoke/instrumental track etc.); composition claims relate to melody/lyrics/structure; recording your own cover creates a new master you own but not the underlying composition — [StemSplit cover guide](https://stemsplit.io/blog/cover-songs-youtube-permission), [Sing2Music](https://www.sing2music.com/covers-on-youtube-and-copyright-claims/)
- Most cover videos stay up with 100% of ad revenue going to rights holders under a claim; a claim is not a strike — [StemSplit](https://stemsplit.io/blog/cover-songs-youtube-permission), [iMusician on claims](https://imusician.pro/en/resources/blog/how-to-handle-youtube-copyright-claims)
- Audio Library tracks are described as safe from Content ID claims, though some third-party claims can still occur — [vidIQ Audio Library guide](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/royalty-free-music-youtube-audio-library/), [YouTube Help: Audio Library](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882?hl=en)
- Labels such as T-Series (Bhakti Sagar) and Saregama Bhakti run large devotional catalogs on YouTube — [T-Series Bhakti Sagar](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCaayLD9i5x4MmIoVZxXSv_g), [Saregama devotional](https://www.saregama.com/devotional)
- Some sites market "copyright free Hanuman Chalisa" tracks; these are not automatically safe (see Hanuman Chalisa claim example above). No source found verifying YouTube's treatment of Google-sourced deity images specifically.

### Inferences
- Google Images pictures of deities (calendar art, modern illustrations, photos of temple idols by photographers) are usually copyrighted; using them can lead to takedown (copyright complaint, DMCA-style strike) rather than Content ID. Use Ravi Varma-era public domain, own photos/AI-free artwork, or licensed stock.
- Avoid "Copyright Disclaimer: fair use" text; India's fair dealing (s.52) is narrow and the disclaimer has no legal effect (general knowledge, not sourced here).
- Practical: keep a folder with proof of your own recording (project files, raw stems), licence certificates and download receipts to dispute wrongful claims.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube Help page could be opened for Content ID or copyright strike rules; no India-specific data on how often labels claim public-domain bhajans recorded by others; no source on the strike penalties in this session.

## 3. Royalty-free / CC sources and caveats

### Takeaway
Best low-risk order: your own recordings, YouTube Audio Library (check licence type column), Pixabay/Pexels for images/video, Wikimedia Commons for public-domain art (check per-file licence), Freesound only CC0/CC BY (never NC for monetized).

### Cited Findings
- Audio Library: "YouTube Audio Library License" tracks need no attribution; a subset are CC BY 4.0 and require crediting the artist/linking in the description; licence type is shown in the Studio Audio Library — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/royalty-free-music-youtube-audio-library/), [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882?hl=en)
- Pixabay music is under the Pixabay Content License, but contributors' distributors may register tracks in Content ID, producing claims even for legal use; dispute with the Pixabay License Certificate (up to 30 days) — [Pixabay blog](https://pixabay.com/blog/posts/how-to-clear-a-youtube-content-id-claim-with-a-pix-190/)
- Pexels: photos/videos free for personal and commercial use without attribution; cannot sell unaltered copies or resell on other stock platforms, cannot imply endorsement by people/brands shown, no use in trademarks — [Pexels license](https://www.pexels.com/license/), [Pexels help](https://help.pexels.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042332714-What-are-the-rules-for-using-Pexels-photos-or-videos)
- Freesound: CC0 (no credit), CC BY (credit required), CC BY-NC (credit and no commercial use; monetized YouTube counts as commercial) — [Freesound FAQ](https://freesound.org/help/faq/), [Freesound forum](https://freesound.org/forum/legal-help-and-attribution-questions/39864/)
- Wikimedia Commons hosts public-domain Raja Ravi Varma deity paintings with per-file licence tags — [Commons example](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Raja_Ravi_Varma_-_Dattatreya.jpg); a Commons "Hanuman showing Rama in His heart" file exists, licence not checked — [Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hanuman_showing_Rama_in_His_heart.jpg)

### Inferences
- Commons: a faithful photograph of a 2D public-domain painting is generally treated as PD, but check each file page (photographer's or country-specific rules can differ). Modern devotional illustrations on Commons may be CC BY-SA (share-alike, attribution needed).
- Pexels/Pixabay images of idols/temples may contain identifiable people or trademarks; avoid faces and brands.
- Not researched: pixabay/pexels licence wording changes, AI-generated stock images licence status.

### Gaps
- Pixabay image licence page and Wikimedia reuse terms were not opened; no source on Indian government/temple image licences (e.g., Wikimedia India collections).

## 4. YouTube Partner Program requirements and inauthentic/reused/mass-produced content

### Takeaway
As of 2026 YPP has two tiers: an entry tier (500 subs) for fan funding and a full ad-revenue tier (1,000 subs plus 4,000 watch hours or 10M Shorts views). A change to higher ad-tier thresholds is reported for Feb 1, 2027 (secondary source). Looped/static/templated audio videos are the classic "inauthentic content" risk, more so for devotional channels using the same image and looped chant.

### Cited Findings
- Tier 1 (early access): 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads (in 90 days per common guides), and 3,000 qualified watch hours (12 months) or 3 million public Shorts views (90 days); unlocks fan funding/Shopping, not ad revenue — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-partner-program-requirements-2026-the-complete-guide), [vidIQ YPP guide](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-guide/), [Google Help overview](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240?hl=en)
- Tier 2 (full ads): 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours or 10 million Shorts views; watch hours and Shorts views cannot be combined — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-partner-program-requirements-2026-the-complete-guide), [YouTube creators page](https://www.youtube.com/creators/partner-program/)
- One secondary source reports Tier 2 thresholds rising on 1 Feb 2027 to 1,000 subs plus 8,000 watch hours (365 days) or 20M Shorts views (90 days) for new applicants — [AIR Media-Tech search summary](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-partner-program-requirements-2026-the-complete-guide). Single/secondary source; contradicted by no source, unverified against YouTube; SubSub headline mentions "2027" too — [SubSub](https://www.subsub.io/blog/youtube-monetization-requirements)
- July 15, 2025: "repetitious content" renamed "inauthentic content": mass-produced or repetitive content following a template with minimal variation, easy to reproduce at scale; YouTube says this was always ineligible, a clarification — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/), [iMusician](https://imusician.pro/en/resources/blog/youtube-updates-its-monetization-policies), [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/youtube-updates-monetisation-policies-ai-and-repetitive-content-ban-begins-july-15-1.500192660)
- Examples described: image slideshows/scrolling text with no narrative or commentary; looping visuals/static images/screensaver-style animations nearly identical across videos; bulk template videos with slight variation; generic AI-template content without creator's original perspective — [SubSub](https://www.subsub.io/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-2025), [Sybrid](https://sybrid.com/resources/blog/youtube-monetization-updates-2025/)
- Ambient/lo-fi/meditation music channels reportedly have some of the highest YPP rejection rates due to long continuous tracks with minimal visual variation — [Dynamoi](https://dynamoi.com/learn/youtube-music-promotion/ambient-music-channel-monetization-rules) (commercial blog; unverified statistic)
- Reused content = others' material without significant original commentary/modification; re-posting is fine with added value. Sources say policy was "clarified" again in July 2026 — [vidIQ reused content](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-reused-content-policy-guide/), [Lenspov](https://lenspov.com/articles/youtube-ai-content-demonetization-2026) (secondary; details of any 2026 change not verified)
- AI voiceover with original script/structure is described as compliant; AI reading a generic/scraped script without human input is not — [Last Play Distro](https://lastplaydistro.com/blog/youtube-reused-content-policy-2026-ai-faceless-videos), [TubeBuddy](https://www.tubebuddy.com/blog/youtube-ai-monetization-best-practices/)
- Policy page: [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en) (not opened)

### Inferences
- Repeating the same static deity image with a looped mantra (e.g., 108x Om Namah Shivaya on a still picture) sits squarely in the flagged pattern. Reduce risk with: unique visuals per video, original vocals or instruments, chant meaning/translation on screen or in voice-over, distinct lyrics-synced visuals, varied video length and structure, intros with creator's voice.
- Long-form "1 hour mantra jaap" videos are not banned per se, but YPP reviewers judge the whole channel; a channel of only near-identical loops is at risk.
- Whether Hindi/regional channels get different review timing: no source; YPP is available in India.
- Monetization also requires clean rights (no Content ID claims eating revenue) and advertiser-friendly content.

### Gaps
- Official YPP page and the exact 2026 policy text were inaccessible; the Feb 2027 threshold change and "July 2026 clarification" rest on secondary sites and need verification. Shorts-specific monetization policy (Shorts revenue pool) not researched.

## 5. Disclosure rules for AI-generated or altered content

### Takeaway
Creators must use the Studio "altered or synthetic content" toggle when content is realistic and could be mistaken for real people, places or events. Clearly unrealistic/animated content, and AI used only for production help, are exempt.

### Cited Findings
- Disclosure required for realistic altered/synthetic content, e.g., face replacement, synthetic voice saying something a person did not say, altered footage of real events/places, realistic fictional major events; policy introduced March 2024 — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content/), [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/ai-disclosure-rules/)
- Not required: clearly unrealistic, animated, special-effects content; generative AI for scripts, ideas, auto-captions — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content/), [Minimatters](https://minimatters.com/youtube-altered-or-synthetic-content-disclosure/)
- One secondary source says using your own cloned voice is exempt, but a synthetic voice/AI visuals presented as real need disclosure — [Lenspov](https://lenspov.com/articles/youtube-ai-content-demonetization-2026), [ShortsFast](https://shortsfast.com/blog/youtube-ai-content-disclosure-rules-2026/) (conflict-prone; verify)
- Related: YouTube's inauthentic-content and likeness rules also touch creator likeness — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/youtube-inauthentic-content/)

### Inferences
- Stylized AI art of a deity is likely "clearly unrealistic" (no disclosure), but a photorealistic AI-generated temple/aarti scene or an AI voice mimicking a known singer (e.g., a famous bhajan singer) would need disclosure and may violate impersonation/likeness and copyright/personality rights. Safe rule: disclose when in doubt; do not clone famous singers' voices.
- AI-generated music/voice is not exempt from inauthentic-content review.

### Gaps
- Exact current toggle wording, label placement (description vs. player) and enforcement penalties were not confirmed from official pages.
